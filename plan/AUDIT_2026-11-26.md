# Audit of the mechanics, relativity, Kerr and dark energy modules (26 November 2026)

The modules added on 25-26 November 2026 were written by one set of authors and then audited by independent readers who had not seen the authors' reports. Each auditor opened the cited TeX at the cited line for every public function, compared the equation with the code term by term, redid the derivations marked "derivation in this project", and probed the functions with calculations of their own. This file records what was found and what was done about it.

## Result

No function implemented an equation wrongly: every formula, sign, factor and index order matched its source or its stated derivation. The defects were of four other kinds.

### Wrong results outside the tested regime

- **Derivative helper (`core/_differentiation`).** The check that refuses non-analytic functions had an absolute tolerance, and the finite-difference steps were fixed at 1e-6 and 1e-3. A Lagrangian written with `norm` and an SI-size mass returned a momentum of 0 without an error; a function varying on a scale of 1e-4 returned a second derivative with a relative error of order one; an analytic function with a length scale of 1e-5 was refused. The helper was rewritten: the check is relative and tries steps from 1e-3 to 1e-9, the finite-difference step is reduced until the two-point and four-point formulas agree, inner steps are frozen inside an outer difference, and the convergence of a central difference near a stationary point is recognized. Tests: `tests/unit/test_differentiation.py` (each new test failed with the first version).
- **Kerr circular orbits.** Energy and angular momentum lost accuracy close to the photon orbit (3e-3 at spin 1, relative distance 1e-7) because a cancelling difference entered squared. They now use the closed form of the angular momentum printed in Pugliese, Quevedo & Ruffini (2011); the loss at the same point is 8e-11, and a radius too close to resolve raises an error.
- **Bouncing cosmologies.** For models in which E² becomes negative at some redshift, the density parameters, the deceleration parameter and the growth functions returned finite numbers; they now return NaN or raise. `acceleration_onset_redshift` could return a pole of q, or the end of an accelerating phase instead of its beginning; it now requires acceleration today and returns where that phase began.
- **Shapiro delay.** The formula as printed in the source cancels when the two end points and the mass are nearly collinear on the same side (relative error 2e-5 at an offset of 1e-6 au) and failed for exact collinearity; an algebraically identical form is evaluated instead.

### Statements that did not match the code

The Legendre inversion limit for the relativistic particle (documented near p = 1000 m, measured 50 m; raised to 2.1e5 m, where the velocity is within 1e-11 of the speed of light, and documented), the sense of the azimuth of the Kerr metric in Kerr's original coordinates (opposite to Boyer-Lindquist, now stated and tested), the accuracy of the eccentricity near zero (square root of machine epsilon, now stated), "zero at the ISCO" for the radial epicyclic frequency, a note that named the wrong spline, and several citations that were off by one to three TeX lines.

### Tests that could not fail

A mutation run on the dark energy modules and a reading of the other test files found tests that passed with a wrong coefficient or compared a function with itself. Added or replaced: the explicit-time term of the Euler-Lagrange equations and of the Legendre Hamiltonian; time-dependent forces for every integrator; component-level Riemann tensor; components and azimuth sense of the second Kerr metric; closed-form Killing defects; radiation in the scalar-field background against an independent integration in cosmic time; growth in curved, phantom and radiation models against an independent integration; the fixed-point classification exactly at its boundaries; the Kerr energy and angular momentum against the printed closed forms; the Kerr epicyclic frequencies against the oscillations of an integrated geodesic. Random tests now own their generators, and their tolerances come from the maximum over 300 seeds.

### Found in the sources

Carroll's notes print the Kretschmann scalar of the Schwarzschild metric as 12 G²M²/r⁶ (Eq. 7.30); the value is 48 G²M²/r⁶. The same notes print (r² + a² cos²θ)² in two components of the Kerr metric (Eq. 7.117), which is dimensionally inconsistent. Reynolds (2021) prints a Lense-Thirring rate that does not have the dimension of a frequency for a dimensionless spin. Linder (2003) writes the dark energy density with exp(−3∫…) in its general expression; its own resolved forms use +3∫.

## What the audit did not cover

`black_holes/basics.py` was used as an oracle and not audited. The constants were not rechecked against their sources. The auditors did not read the papers beyond the cited passages. No mutation run was made on the mechanics, relativity or Kerr tests; "could not fail" was judged there by reading and by probes. The literature values of the next-order terms of the periapsis advance and of the light deflection were not read in a source: the tests extrapolate the measured coefficients (4.5023 and 2.9452) and assert only that the extrapolation is stable.

## Second round (26 November 2026, after the first fixes)

The first round ended with a rewritten derivative helper that nobody had audited, tests whose strength had been measured in one package only, and `black_holes/basics.py` unaudited. Three more independent pieces of work closed those gaps.

### The derivative helper, audited again

An adversarial audit of the rewritten helper found that it still returned wrong numbers without an error far from unit scale. Reproduced and confirmed here before any change:

- A planet in SI units at (7.8e11 m, 0): the acceleration along y came out as 3.4e-5 m/s² instead of 0 (15 % of the total), because a function that is exactly constant gave a non-zero four-point difference by rounding, at a step of 1e-3 m in a problem of size 1e11 m.
- A black hole of 1e9 solar masses in metres: Kretschmann scalar wrong by 23 % at 3.7 M; cosmic time in seconds in an FLRW metric: wrong by many orders of magnitude.
- A Gaussian of width 1e-6 and a Coulomb potential at 5e-11 m: derivative returned as 0, because the stencil sampled only the far tails.
- A small non-analytic term next to a smooth slope (1e-3 x + 4e-7 |x|) passed the check; frozen inner steps were keyed by code object and collided; the bookkeeping was shared between threads.
- The Legendre inversion returned H = 0 for an electron in SI units; the singularity test of the inertia matrix refused the polar Kepler problem in SI units.

The helper was redesigned rather than patched (`core/_differentiation.py`, module docstring): every finite difference now carries an error estimate (noise, rounding of the step, truncation, roughness), the step is searched over twenty-one decades and chosen element by element, a larger step is used only if it agrees with the result of the smaller ones, and a result whose estimate exceeds the limit is refused. The accelerations propagate the estimates and raise `DerivativePrecisionError`; the Riemann tensor compares them in an orthonormal frame and raises `CurvaturePrecisionError`. Every reproduction above is now a test, and each either gives the right number (planet 1e-16, polar Kepler 4e-14, electron 1e-13, Kretschmann 2e-13 ... 3e-10 from 4 M to 1e4 M in either unit system) or is refused.

What remains outside its reach, as measured: a non-analytic part whose derivative is below about 2e-11 of the function value per unit of the argument; structure finer than a hundredth of the first step with an amplitude below 1e-10 of the function value; a curvature more than about twelve orders of magnitude below the connection terms (returned as zero with a warning); the Legendre inversion of the relativistic particle beyond p = 1.14e4 m. Cost, measured for two coordinates: a single acceleration with its checks takes 210 evaluations of the Lagrangian with the complex step and 2369 with the finite method; inside an integration, where the steps are reused, about 66 and about 860.

### Mutation testing of the mechanics, relativity and Kerr tests

522 hand-written mutants (flipped signs, factors of two, swapped indices and arguments, changed boundaries), one at a time: 458 were detected (87.7 %; 89.5 % without the ten survivors judged equivalent). The 54 real survivors were of three kinds: untested boundary and validation branches, constants of numerical heuristics, and genuine gaps (an unweighted barycentre, the sign of the angular momentum vector, the explicit-time term with more than one coordinate, a generator nonlinear in q, a batch of velocities). One cause reached further than any single test: `pytest.approx(x, rel=...)` keeps an absolute tolerance of 1e-12, so comparisons of small SI quantities (1e-27 kg, 1e-14 rad/s) could not fail; a mutant that returned the invariant mass wrong by a factor of c survived for that reason. Every such call in the test tree now carries `abs=0`, and tests were added for the survivors.

### `black_holes/basics.py`, `accretion.py` and the constants

All 36 functions matched their sources and independent calculations (ISCO against a 60-digit root of dE/dr = 0, the photon orbit against the cubic, horizon and ergosphere against the Kerr metric, Hawking quantities and gravitational-wave formulas against hand calculations); the constants matched CODATA 2022 and the IAU 2015 nominal values. Corrected: a section number in a citation, the author list of Bardeen, Press & Teukolsky (1972), two docstring numbers, missing validation of the species counts of the Hawking rate, the Wien constant (stored truncated, now derived), and five tests that could not tell the cases apart.

### State after the second round

3595 tests collected; 3594 passed and 1 skipped in file order (507 s), 3588 passed, 1 skipped and 6 network tests deselected in random order (432 s), both after the fix described below. `ruff check` and `ruff format --check` clean. 414 functions, 476 source records, all read; no undefined citation.

Found while measuring that cost, after an earlier pair of full runs had passed (3592 and 3586 tests): with `method="finite"` the second derivatives of an ordinary Lagrangian at unit scale (two coupled pendula) were wrong in 13 of 120 elements at random states, the worst by 2.8. The error estimate flagged every one, so no wrong acceleration was returned, but accelerations were refused without need and an integration of one unit of time did not finish in 100 s. Cause: the outer difference sees a numerical derivative whose noise is far above rounding; one sixth difference can show that noise too small by a factor of 45, and the search anchored on a small step that was all noise. Fixed in `core/_differentiation.py`: the largest sixth difference met at a step or below it is a floor for the noise at that step, and the noise part of the estimates counts a hundredfold when two steps are compared. Measured afterwards on 600 elements: worst error 6.5e-10, none refused; the integration takes 7 s and differs from the complex step by 1.7e-10. Two tests keep this. Neither the audits nor the mutation run had exercised the finite method on a coupled system away from its initial state.

### Eighth round (same day): an adversarial probe of the mechanics foundations

An independent agent was given one task: make the mechanics modules return a wrong number without an error. It compared 17 systems with hand-derived momenta, forces, inertia matrices, accelerations and Jacobi functions at 200 random states each, in eight systems of units from 1e-30 to 1e30, with both methods, and probed the integrators, the Hamiltonian and Noether functions, the energy functions and the input validation. Every reproduction below was run again here before anything was changed, and again afterwards.

What held: with the complex step, in units of order one and in SI units of a planet and of a galaxy, 4011 states per scale, the worst error of an acceleration was 3e-8 and of a first derivative 1e-13; Hamiltonians by Legendre transformation, Poisson brackets and Noether charges to 2e-14, 1.4e-14 and 5e-15; the symplectic integrators at their orders (2.00 and 4.00), reversible to 4e-13; the energy functions to 5.7e-13 against an 80-digit reference.

What did not (all silent, all confirmed here):

1. **Complex step of absolute size 1e-30.** For L = v^2/2 - q^4/4 the force at q = 1e-30 was 0 (exact -1e-90) and 11 % off at 3e-30. The step is now 1e-30 of the coordinate.
2. **Analyticity tested at the first and the last state only.** A Lagrangian with np.abs on a part of the path that neither end lies in was integrated with a wrong force (error 1.0 in q). The test is made at every output time now, and the docstring says what remains: a non-analytic stretch that no output time falls into.
3. **Legendre inversion converged against the largest momentum.** With momenta of 1e12 and 1.3 the second velocity was 1 % off, and with it dH/dp and the equations of motion; H itself was right to rounding. Every component is judged against its own momentum now.
4. **Noether: an absolute threshold of 1e-12** decided whether a generator depends on the velocity. A generator -1e-13 v lost its acceleration term (variation wrong by a factor of two). Any non-zero dependence needs the acceleration now.
5. **Finite method, first derivatives:** the error of a step was accepted against |f| / h, the size of the function, instead of the size of its variation. With a constant of 1e12 in the Lagrangian the momentum 2 came out as 2.02; with a potential of 1e10 a momentum of 1e-4 came out as -6e-5. The allowance is now taken from the variation of f over the stencil, less its noise; what cannot be determined is refused by the functions that return no estimate.
6. **Finite method, an acceleration wrong by a factor of 300** (spherical Kepler problem at one of 200 states of order one). A momentum that does not depend on a coordinate was recognised as such, and then the quotient of its rounding residues by the smallest step was returned as its derivative. It is zero now.
7. **Finite method, inertia matrix** of the Cartesian Kepler problem with off-diagonal elements of 0.023 m in SI units and 313 m in galactic units (exact 0): the same two causes.
8. **virial_ratio far from the origin** lost accuracy as eps D / d. W is formed about the barycentre now; what remains is the precision of the positions themselves, and the docstring states it.
9. **Overflow:** two bodies 1e160 m apart gave a potential energy of -0.0, and an escape speed at 1e-320 m gave inf. Both are refused.
10. **Masses:** kinetic energy, total momentum and barycentre velocity accepted zero and negative masses. They refuse them like the other functions.

The rule that came out of 5 to 7, in `core/_differentiation.py`: the functions that return an error estimate never refuse, they return the best step and its estimate, and their callers judge (the accelerations and the Riemann tensor propagate it); the functions that return the derivative alone accept an element only if a step determines it, or if its error is below 1e-7 of the largest element that is determined. The test of the complex step keeps its old, looser reference (judged against the size of f), because what it must find is a contradiction, and a test that cannot be made still counts as a refusal: with a best-effort reference the kink of |x| at 0 passed, and that was withdrawn within the hour.

Consequences that were measured: the planet in SI units and the oscillator with v = 5e5, which the finite method had refused, are now right to 8e-14 and exact; an oscillator in SI units, which `integrate_lagrangian` had refused, is integrated; the grid of 480 analytic functions is accepted in the same 426 cases as before; the finite-method integration of two coupled pendula stays below the limit of its test, 3e5 evaluations (the plan of steps is judged against the largest element of the whole Jacobian; with a judgement per column it was rejected at every right-hand side and the count was 4.6e5). The Legendre inversion reached p = 5.6e7 m while the test of analyticity was best-effort, and is back at 1.14e4 m with the conservative one.

Not acted on from the probe's notes: with units of 1e-30 for everything, or an electron in SI units, many states are refused by the complex step ("could not be checked", sometimes worded "not analytic"), because the finite-difference test works with steps relative to max(1, |x|) and a time scale of 1e-17 s is far below them; the finite method refuses 428 of 4000 states of order one, and a mass matrix with a condition number above about 2e2 (the complex step from about 2e6); a NaN in the coordinates is reported as "could not be checked". These are refusals, not wrong numbers. Not covered by the probe: the Hamiltonian and Noether functions with the finite method, Noether charges along integrated orbits, many-body Lagrangians.

### Ninth round (same day): mutation run on the foundations after the redesign

A second independent agent ran 372 hand-written mutants, one at a time and on copies of the repository, against the tests of the seven modules: `core/_differentiation.py` 137 (73 detected), `lagrangian.py` 48 (33), `hamiltonian.py` 44 (33), `noether.py` 37 (35), `energy.py` 43 (37), `integrators.py` 34 (33), `curvature.py` 29 (16). In all 260 detected, 112 not (70 %). The copies were taken before the eighth round, so the run describes the code of `577592e`; five of the seven files changed afterwards.

Of the 112 survivors the agent judged 29 equivalent, 36 constants of heuristics (thresholds, prefactors of the error estimate, the number of decades searched) and 46 real gaps; one more is detected by a test file outside the list it was run against. The judgements were made by reading, with a distinguishing run for seven of them. The formulas themselves (the stencils, the Euler-Lagrange and Hamilton equations, the Noether charges, the energies, the integrators) lost almost no mutant; the gaps were in three places: branches that refuse (shape checks, the analyticity test of the public functions, a failed integration), the error estimates that are returned, and the bookkeeping of nested and reused steps.

`tests/unit/test_foundations_guards.py` (14 tests) pins the ones that change what the library returns or refuses. Seven of their target mutants were applied to the present code to see the tests fail: six did (a vector function with one undetermined element, the safety factor on the plan-reuse path, the symmetric error of a Hessian, the analyticity test of the Riemann tensor, a generator nonlinear in the velocity, a self term in the potential energy). One did not: the orthonormal-frame weighting of the precision decision of the Riemann tensor can be replaced by |g| without any test failing, and no case was found where the decision differs.

Not pinned, and listed so that nobody assumes otherwise: the 36 heuristic constants; the roughness term of the error estimate; the classification of rounding residues; the one-sided parts of the test for independence of a coordinate; frozen inner steps that differ between elements; the fall-back when the inner call sequence changes; the one-sided column of the Newton matrix of the Legendre inversion; the orthonormal-frame weighting. A second mutation run on the code after the eighth round was not made.

### Tenth round (same day): a second probe, of what the first had not covered

A third independent agent probed the Hamiltonian and Noether functions with the finite method, Legendre transformations harder than quadratic, Noether charges along integrated orbits, Lagrangians of 3 to 8 bodies, small non-analytic terms, and use from several threads. Each reproduction was run again here before and after the changes.
